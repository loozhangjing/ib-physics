# Archimedes' principle

>[!quote] Definition
>The buoyant force exerted on a body immersed in fluid is equal to the weight of fluid that the body displaces.
$$F_B = W_\text{displaced fluid}$$

>[!note]- Notes
>- Buoyant force always acts upwards.
>- A body can be immersed partially or fully in the fluid.
## Mass from density & volume
Weight is often calculated with mass. \[$W = mg$]

In problems involving buoyant force, however, the density, $\rho$ and volume, $V$ of the displaced fluid are more often given than its mass.

Since density is the mass per unit volume ($\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$), the mass of the displaced fluid is its density multiplied by its volume.
$$m = \rho V$$
Therefore,
$$W = \rho V g$$
Applying Archimedes' principle,
$$F_B = \rho V g$$
# Float or sink?
The body floats (remains vertically stationary) when the net force on it is zero, i.e. when its weight equals the buoyant force.
## Partial static immersion
For a body that floats but is not fully immersed in a fluid, the above still applies:
$$\begin{aligned}
W_\text{body} &= F_B\\
\rho_\text{body} V_\text{body} g &= \rho_\text{fluid}V_\text{fluid} g
\end{aligned}$$
The weight of the body is the weight of the *whole* body, not just the part that's immersed! That's why the volumes of the body and fluid are also different, with $V_\text{fluid} < V_\text{body}$.
## Full static immersion
Since $V_\text{body}$ is now the same as $V_\text{fluid}$ (and $g = g$, of course),
$$\rho_\text{body} = \rho_\text{fluid}$$
