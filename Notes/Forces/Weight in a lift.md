# Lift moving upwards
$$F_\text{net} = R - mg$$
Since $F_\text{net} = ma$ \[Newton's Second Law],
$$R = mg + ma$$
# On normal force, $R$
Normal force is **not** always equal to the weight of a body.

When a body is in a lift accelerating upwards, $R > W$ because the lift exerts additional force on the body.

> [!info] Weighing scales measure *normal force*, not weight!
> This means that, if a body rests on a weighing scale in a lift accelerating upwards, the value of the scale reading exceeds the body's weight.
