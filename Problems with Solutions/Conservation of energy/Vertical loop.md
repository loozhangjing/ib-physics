# Question
In a “loop-the-loop” toy, a car of mass $\pu{0.12 kg}$ is released from rest. The initial position of the car is $\pu{45 cm}$ above level ground. The radius of the circular loop is $\pu{15 cm}$. The car reaches the top of the loop at position P. Frictional and air resistance forces are negligible.
![[Vertical loop.png]]
## Show that the speed of the car at P is $\pu{1.7 m s^-1}$.
Energy is conserved, therefore the gravitational potential energy of the car at its initial position is the same as the sum of its kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy at position P. (The car has no kinetic energy at its initial position because it is at rest.)
$$\begin{aligned}
mgh_\text{initial} &= \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + mgh_P\\
gh_\text{initial} &= \dfrac{1}{2}v^2 + gh_P\\
v &= \sqrt{2 (gh_\text{initial} - gh_P)}\\
v &= \sqrt{2 (9.8 \cdot \dfrac{45}{100} - 9.8 \cdot \dfrac{2 \cdot 15}{100})}\\
v &= \pu{1.7 m s^-1}
\end{aligned}$$
